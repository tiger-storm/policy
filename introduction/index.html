Introduction

Introducing Now Launcher

A home screen that starts with what you need, when you need it.

Now Launcher began with a small, recurring frustration: picking up my phone to do something simple, then spending time finding where to start. An app on another page. A widget a few swipes away. Something I knew was there, but still had to look for.

Each interaction was small. Together, they made the home screen feel like another thing to navigate before I could get on with what I wanted to do. I wanted a shorter path from picking up my phone to finding something useful.

From apps to relevant suggestions

A traditional home screen starts with the apps you have installed. You arrange them into a grid, put some into folders, add a few widgets, and learn where everything lives. Over time, using your phone becomes partly an exercise in remembering that arrangement.

The starting point for Now was a different question: what would be useful right now?

That question shifts the emphasis from showing a collection of apps to surfacing relevant suggestions. An app is still useful, but its place on the screen should reflect when you might need it. The same thinking applies to information: an upcoming event can be more useful than the calendar icon that leads to it.

The aim is to make the next step easier to spot. You should be able to open your home screen, recognise something useful, and act on it without first translating what you want into a place to look.

Less time looking

Adding another home screen page gives you more room, but it also gives you another place to search. Put a widget on one page and an app on another, and even a familiar task can begin with a few swipes back and forth.

I wanted to remove as much of that searching as possible. Scrolling has a purpose when you are exploring something. It feels less useful when you are simply trying to reach an app or check a piece of information you already know you need.

For Now, reducing those repeated movements became a design constraint. Every extra page or layer needed a reason to exist. The home screen should help you reach your destination with fewer detours.

One panel, a clear starting point

The idea was to centralise the experience into one panel: a consistent place for apps, information and suggestions. Bringing them together means you have one starting point, with less need to remember which page holds which thing.

Of course, putting everything together can create its own clutter. A single panel only helps if it is easy to read. That made a minimal, clean interface essential: clear hierarchy, enough space to distinguish things, and restraint about what competes for attention.

My measure of simplicity was practical. Could I understand what was on screen quickly? Could I find the next action without scanning a wall of equally prominent items? A cleaner layout needed to make those moments easier.

Speed is part of the experience

Fewer swipes would mean little if the panel felt slow. Fast processing and smooth animation were requirements from the beginning. Opening the launcher, bringing up information and moving between states all needed to feel responsive.

Motion has a job here. It should make a change easy to follow, showing how the interface responds to your touch. Transitions should feel connected and controlled, with no unnecessary pause before you can take the next step.

That is the slickness I wanted: quick responses and movement that feels natural. The time saved by a more direct layout should carry through the whole interaction.

A home screen for now

Now Launcher brings these ideas together around a simple ambition: spend less time finding your way around your phone. Relevant suggestions give you a useful starting point. One panel reduces the places you need to look. A clean interface and responsive motion help you move through it comfortably.

That is why I created Now: to make the moment between knowing what you want and getting to it a little shorter, every time you pick up your phone.
